title: A metre of deep snow — keep it moving, keep the nose up, keep it upright
---

The free ride's **SNOW** row is now a depth of snow — **THIN** 20 cm, **MEDIUM** 40 cm (the snow every race is ridden on), **THICK** 70 cm and a new **VERY DEEP**, a metre of fresh snow as it lies in a northern forest by March. Past MEDIUM the powder is **bottomless**, and it rides the way riders say deep snow rides: a sled that stops sinks down to its belly and stays sunk; pinned from a standstill a crossover only wallows through it, and leaning back to lift the nose is what gets it up onto the top (the mountain sled planes on its own; the trail and touring sleds never do); let off the throttle while planing and it sinks back in. Down in it the sled **rides like a bike**: the buried skis hold nothing and the loaded side gives, so across a slope it rolls over unless you hang your weight uphill with the bars — and hang it too far and it goes over the other way. The furrow a sled leaves is drawn as deep as it sinks, down to a metre.

The rider also **hangs off only as far as the bend needs** on the groomed track: at a crawl he sits in the middle whatever the bars say, and he is fully over from about 20 km/h at full lock. His weight is a real moment on the machine, so the lean you see is the lean that acts.
