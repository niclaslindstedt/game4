title: A snow cloud, and six kinds of snow
---

Every sled now rips up a real cloud of snow behind it: a rooster tail off the track that rises, stalls, billows, drifts on the wind and hangs over the line it cut, lit by the sky — white on the sunny side, blue-grey where it shades itself, glowing against a low sun with crystals glinting in it, and red behind a taillight at night — and the chase camera sees through your own tail to the sled. How much snow flies, and what it does, depends on the snow: a thin mist of ice dust off the groomed track, chunks off a wind crust, a big lingering cloud out of soft powder, the biggest and finest out of new snow, heavy clumps and hardly any cloud out of wet spring snow, nothing off ice. The same snow shapes every track in it: deep furrows with sloughed-in walls in new snow, clean square cuts in crust, crisp walls and a berm in wet snow — and a fox walks on a crust a moose breaks through.
