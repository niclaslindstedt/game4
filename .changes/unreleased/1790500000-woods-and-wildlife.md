title: Twenty kinds of tree in ten shapes each, woods you can see into, and more wildlife
---

The woods are no longer one spruce copied: there are now **twenty kinds of tree** — spruce, fir, pine, larch, black spruce, stone pine, white pine, lodgepole, hemlock, juniper, dwarf pine and grey dead snags among the conifers, and birch, aspen, rowan (its red berries still on it), alder, willow (a thicket of orange twigs), beech (keeping its copper leaves all winter), maple and ash bare for the winter — each country growing its own mix (a northern forest mostly spruce and pine; the high alpine fir, larch and stone pine; the tundra stunted spruce, mountain birch and willow scrub; the birch valley birch, aspen and alder). Every kind comes in **ten shapes**: a dense spire, a narrow pencil, a snow ghost bowed under its load, a stand tree bare underneath, a storm-broken top, a black spruce's club top, a hemlock's nodding leader, a white pine's flat layers, a dwarf pine sprawling on five stems, a birch with three stems off one root, and more. On a free ride, a link's own seed and every new map, the woods now **bunch into clumps** with open ground between and have **lanes** winding through them, so you see well into a wood and there is always a way between two groups of trees; the campaign's maps keep the woods they were curated with. And more lives in them: **red squirrels, roe deer, a wolverine and a wolf pack** in the boreal forest, **black grouse** and **snow buntings** that burst off the snow as you pass, a **black woodpecker** and a **great grey owl** — and in the open country the **arctic fox**, **chamois** on the alpine faces and **musk oxen** on the tundra. OPTIONS ▸ PICTURE ▸ FOREST also sets how many different tree shapes are drawn at once, so a slower device keeps its frame rate.
